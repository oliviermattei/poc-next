This page walks you, once and in order, through what your clients will see and what you will see. Allow about twenty minutes. You need a running application: if that is not the case yet, follow [Install the project](/en/demarrer/installer) first.

Your SaaS has three areas, served by **a single application** on `http://localhost:3000`:

| Area | Address | Who gets in |
|---|---|---|
| **The public site** | `/`, `/pricing`, `/blog`… | everyone |
| **The application** | `/app` and below | a signed-in client |
| **The console** | `/console` and below | you, as superadmin |

<Callout type="info" title="The language prefix">
The site is served in French and in English. When you type an address such as `http://localhost:3000/pricing`, you are redirected to `http://localhost:3000/fr/pricing` (or `/en/pricing`, depending on your browser’s language). That is expected. On this page, addresses are written without this prefix.
</Callout>

## The demo accounts

The `pnpm db:seed` command created two accounts, already verified: you can sign in right away, with no email to read.

| Address | Password | What this account has |
|---|---|---|
| `ada@demonstration.invalid` | `demonstration-seulement` | owner of the “Atelier Démo” organization, an active subscription to the `pro-monthly` offer, notifications |
| `hedy@demonstration.invalid` | `demonstration-seulement` | member of the same organization |

These addresses cannot receive any mail (the `.invalid` domain is reserved for that purpose), and their password is public. They cannot reach a database in service: the seed refuses to run as soon as the database holds an account it did not create.

## 1. The public site

<Steps>
<Step>

### The home page

Open `http://localhost:3000`. You should see the home page, made of sections: a header with the sign-up and sign-in buttons, features, testimonials, frequently asked questions, newsletter sign-up, call to action. Their order is set in `config/marketing.ts` (see [Site and legal pages](/en/configurer/site-et-pages-legales)).

</Step>
<Step>

### Pricing

Open `/pricing`. You should see the three offers shipped as examples in `config/billing.ts`: a monthly subscription at €29, a yearly subscription at €290 (both with a 14-day trial) and a one-time purchase at €490. You will replace them with your own (see [Offers and prices](/en/configurer/offres-et-prix)).

</Step>
<Step>

### Content and the other pages

Open in turn:

- `/blog`, `/docs` (the product documentation, for your clients) and `/changelog`;
- `/legal/privacy` and `/legal/terms`, the legal pages;
- `/contact` and `/waitlist`.

Each address should show its page. The content is an example, to be replaced with yours.

</Step>
</Steps>

## 2. The application, as a client sees it

<Steps>
<Step>

### Sign in

Open `/sign-in` and sign in with `ada@demonstration.invalid` and the password `demonstration-seulement`. You land on `/app`, the dashboard. If an onboarding course remains to be done for this account, the application takes you there first: follow it or skip its optional steps.

</Step>
<Step>

### The dashboard

The dashboard shows “Nothing to show yet”. It is **empty on purpose**: this is where your product will plug in. The boilerplate ships everything around your product, never the product itself.

</Step>
<Step>

### Notifications

Open `/app/notifications`. You should see the account’s demo notifications.

</Step>
<Step>

### Settings

Open `/app/settings/profile`. The settings menu gives access to:

| Page | Address | What you do there |
|---|---|---|
| Profile | `/app/settings/profile` | name, email address, avatar, data export, account deletion |
| Security | `/app/settings/security` | password, sign-in methods, active sessions, two-factor authentication, passkeys |
| Organization | `/app/settings/organization` | the current organization, its settings, create one, delete it |
| Members | `/app/settings/members` | members and invitations |
| Billing | `/app/settings/billing` | the subscription, the available offers, the purchases |
| Notifications | `/app/settings/notifications` | notification preferences |
| Cookies | `/app/settings/cookies` | consent choices |

</Step>
<Step>

### A feature reserved for a paid offer

Open `/app/premium`. Since Ada has an active subscription, you should see “Your detailed report”. This screen comes from the demo module: it shows how to reserve a feature for some offers (`config/gating.ts`, see [Reserved features](/en/configurer/fonctionnalites-reservees)).

</Step>
<Step>

### Sign up as a new client

Sign out (account menu, “Sign out” entry), then open `/sign-up` and create an account with an address of your choice. The application sends a verification email. Locally, it is not sent: it is written in the `apps/web/.mail/` folder of the repository, as an `.html` file. Open that file in your browser and click the link: your account is verified.

To try the simulated payment, this new account can choose an offer on `/pricing`: the checkout is simulated, no payment takes place.

</Step>
</Steps>

## 3. The console, your back office

The console is for you, the publisher of the SaaS: to look at accounts, organizations, revenue and sign-ups. It answers **404 to everyone** as long as no superadmin exists. The first one is designated by an environment variable.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Designate the first superadmin

Open your `.env` file, at the root of the repository, and fill in the address of the account that will administer the platform:

```bash
SUPERADMIN_EMAIL=ada@demonstration.invalid
```

The account must already exist. For your real SaaS, sign up first with your address, then put it here.

</Step>
<Step>

### Restart the application

Variables are read at startup. In the terminal running `pnpm dev`, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd>, then start again:

```bash
pnpm dev
```

The warning about `SUPERADMIN_EMAIL` should no longer appear.

</Step>
<Step>

### Open the console

Signed in as Ada, type the address `http://localhost:3000/console`: no link in the application leads there. You should see the console, with its dashboard and a sidebar: **Dashboard**, **Accounts**, **Organizations**, **Revenue**, **Subscriptions**.

Signed in with another account, the same address answers 404, like a page that does not exist: the console does not reveal that it exists.

</Step>
<Step>

### Tour the console

- **Accounts** (`/console/users`): the list of accounts. An account’s detail lets you send a password reset and revoke a session.
- **Organizations** (`/console/organizations`): each organization, its headcount and its subscription.
- **Revenue** (`/console/revenue`): the estimated recurring revenue and the one-time purchases collected.
- **Subscriptions** (`/console/subscriptions`): the addresses left on the public site (newsletter and waitlist).

</Step>
</Steps>

Once the first superadmin is designated, **the database is the reference**: changing `SUPERADMIN_EMAIL` no longer designates anyone else.

<Callout type="warn" title="What is not included">
- **Your product**: the application’s dashboard is empty, it is yours to fill (see [Work with an AI assistant](/en/demarrer/travailler-avec-une-ia) and [Features](/en/fonctionnalites)).
- **Banning an account, appointing another superadmin or signing in as a client from the console screen**: these actions exist on the server, but the screen does not offer them yet ([issue #135](https://github.com/oliviermattei/poc-next/issues/135)).
- **Charts in the console dashboard**: it shows totals, with no change over time ([issue #181](https://github.com/oliviermattei/poc-next/issues/181)).
- **Configuring the SaaS from the console**: configuration lives in `config/` and `.env`, and changes through a redeployment.
- **Logs and usage statistics**: they are read in [Sentry](/en/services/sentry) and [PostHog](/en/services/posthog), not in the console.
</Callout>
