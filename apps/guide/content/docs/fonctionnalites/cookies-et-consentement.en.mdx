## In one sentence

The `consent` module stops your SaaS from loading a third-party service that sets cookies (audience measurement, for example) until the visitor has accepted it, and lets them change their mind at any time.

## What your customers see

- **A banner** “Non-essential cookies”, with three buttons: “Accept all”, “Refuse all”, “Customise my choices”. It only appears **when there is something to accept**.
- **A preferences screen** at the `/cookies` address, one box per purpose: “Audience measurement” and “Advertising”. Only the purposes your site actually uses appear there.
- **Two ways back to it**: the “Cookies” link in the site footer, and the “Cookies” section of the Settings (`/app/settings/cookies`) for a signed-in customer — this second way exists even if you disable the public site.

Everything works without JavaScript: refusing cookies depends on no script.

**Nothing loads by default.** A purpose the visitor has not decided on authorises nothing. The choice is kept for six months in the `app_consent` cookie, on the visitor's device; it is never stored in the database. Strictly necessary cookies (session, language, this choice itself) are not subject to the choice.

### When does the banner appear?

In the shipped project, a single third-party service goes through this consent: the PostHog audience measurement of the [Analytics](/en/fonctionnalites/analytics) module. So the banner appears when:

1. the `analytics` module is enabled, **and**
2. `POSTHOG_KEY` and `POSTHOG_HOST` are set.

Otherwise there is nothing to accept: no banner, no cookie, and `/cookies` shows “No non-essential cookies”. This is the shipped state.

## Enable or disable

```bash
pnpm ks toggle consent
```

The `analytics` module depends on `consent`: as long as it is enabled, disabling `consent` is refused, naming it. Disable `analytics` first.

Module disabled: `/cookies` answers 404, no banner, no non-essential script loaded, no consent cookie set.

## Configure

There is nothing to configure for consent itself. The texts of the banner and of the screen are in `packages/modules/consent/src/messages/fr.json` and `en.json`.

If you add a third-party service that sets cookies yourself, it must be declared in the list of non-essential scripts in `apps/web/lib/consent.ts`, with its purpose (`analytics` or `advertising`): this list is what makes the banner appear and holds the script back until agreement. Its network addresses are also declared in `config/security.ts` (see [Security](/en/configurer/securite)).

## Check that it works

1. Set up audience measurement as described on the [PostHog](/en/services/posthog) page, then restart `pnpm dev`.
2. Open http://localhost:3000 in a private browsing window. You should see the banner at the bottom of the page.
3. Click “Refuse all”. The banner should disappear, and no call to the PostHog host should show up in the network tab of the browser's developer tools.
4. Click the “Cookies” link in the footer: “Audience measurement” should show “Refused”. Tick it and save: it should switch to “Accepted”.

<Callout title="What is not included">
- **No server-side consent register**: the choice lives in the visitor's browser, and nothing keeps a record of who accepted what.
- **No purposes** other than “Audience measurement” and “Advertising”.
- **No advertising service is shipped**: the “Advertising” purpose only appears if you declare such a script.
- **No external consent management provider**: everything is in the product.
</Callout>
