## In one sentence

The `rate-limit` module counts the requests received by sensitive entry points (sign-in, sign-up, password reset, public forms, payment…) and politely refuses those beyond a threshold, to protect your clients against account theft by repeated attempts and your product against abuse.

## What your clients see

Nothing, as long as they stay under the thresholds. Beyond, the form shows for instance "Too many attempts. Try again in 3 minutes.". The wait shown comes from the server’s answer (an HTTP 429 response with the `Retry-After` header), rounded up to the minute.

How it works, in short:

- **Each threshold counts over a window** of time (for instance 5 minutes for sign-in, 1 hour for sign-up).
- **Two counters per entry point**: one per **caller** (their IP address), generous, and one per **targeted account** (the email address entered), tight. The second one stops an attack spread over thousands of IP addresses.
- **The counter is stored in your PostgreSQL database** (table `rate_limit_window`): it is shared by every instance of the application.
- **If the database does not answer, rate limiting refuses** instead of letting requests through.
- **Every public route** of the modules is covered automatically, with the `default` policy when it names no other. Routes reserved to signed-in accounts are covered when they declare a policy (data export, upload…).

## Enable or disable

`rate-limit` belongs to the **core**: it cannot be disabled, and `pnpm ks toggle rate-limit` refuses. **No environment variable disables rate limiting**, and a zero threshold is refused at startup rather than read as "no limit".

## Configure

Thresholds are set in `config/security.ts`, object `rateLimitPolicies`. Each policy has three values:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `windowSeconds` | the length of the window, in seconds |
| `maxPerClient` | the number of passes allowed per caller in the window |
| `maxPerSubject` | the number of passes allowed per targeted account, whatever the origin; `null` when the route targets no account |

Shipped values for the most common ones:

| Policy | Window | Per caller | Per targeted account |
|---|---|---|---|
| `signIn` (sign-in) | 5 min | 300 | 20 |
| `signUp` (sign-up) | 1 h | 120 | 5 |
| `passwordReset`, `magicLink` | 1 h | 120 | 5 |
| `twoFactor` (two-factor code) | 5 min | 60 | 4 |
| `invitation` | 1 h | 120 | 5 |
| `publicForm` (contact, newsletter, waitlist) | 10 min | 60 | — |
| `dataExport` (data export) | 1 h | 20 | — |
| `webhook` (Stripe, etc.) | 1 min | 600 | — |
| `default` (any other public route) | 1 min | 120 | — |

The full list, with the reason for each value, is in the comments of `config/security.ts`. To raise a threshold, change only the number, then restart the application. The [Security](/en/configurer/securite) page details this file.

<Callout type="warn" title="Behind a proxy, the IP address must be trustworthy">
The per-caller counter reads the `x-forwarded-for` header, which anyone can write. In production, put the application behind a reverse proxy that **overwrites** this header. Otherwise, every visitor shares a single per-caller counter (see [Docker and Coolify](/en/deployer/docker-et-coolify)). The per-account counter does not depend on any header.
</Callout>

## Check that it works

1. Open `http://localhost:3000/en/sign-in`.
2. Enter the same address and a wrong password 21 times in a row (the per-account threshold is 20 per 5-minute window).
3. On the 21st attempt, you should see "Too many attempts. Try again in … minutes." instead of the usual refusal.

To clear the counters locally, wait for the window to end: a scheduled job erases closed windows every 10 minutes, and each request also cleans up along the way.

<Callout type="info" title="What is not included">
- **No captcha**: it is declared in `config/security.ts` (`captcha`, disabled) but no provider is shipped ([#151](https://github.com/oliviermattei/poc-next/issues/151)).
- **Routes served outside the modules are not limited**: the `/api/health` health probe, the `/api/csp-report` report collector, and three test routes that answer 404 in production.
- **No setting for the number of trusted proxies**: the caller is read from the first hop of `x-forwarded-for` ([#78](https://github.com/oliviermattei/poc-next/issues/78)).
- Some forms of the application do not show the wait message correctly yet ([#110](https://github.com/oliviermattei/poc-next/issues/110)).
</Callout>
